## 1. Definitions needed to apply the rules

### 1.3 Classification of airplanes — p.1–2 (PDF 0–1)
- **Class IV** covers high-maneuverability airplanes such as Fighter/interceptor, Attack, Tactical reconnaissance, Observation, and Trainer for Class IV.
- Other classes, listed for completeness:
  - Class I: small, light airplanes.
  - Class II: medium-weight, low-to-medium maneuverability airplanes.
  - Class III: large, heavy, low-to-medium maneuverability airplanes.
- The procuring activity assigns the Class. "When no Class is specified in a requirement, the requirement shall apply to all Classes."
- 1.3.1: the suffix -L means land-based and -C means carrier-based. If a requirement makes no such distinction, it applies to both.

### 1.4 Flight Phase Categories — p.2–3 (PDF 1–2)
If no Flight Phase or Category is stated, the requirement applies to all three Categories.
- **Category A** (nonterminal): phases that need rapid maneuvering, precision tracking, or precise flight-path control.
  - Phases: air-to-air combat (CO), ground attack (GA), weapon delivery/launch (WD), aerial recovery (AR), reconnaissance (RC), in-flight refueling as receiver (RR), terrain following (TF), antisubmarine search (AS), close formation flying (FF).
- **Category B** (nonterminal): gradual maneuvers without precision tracking, though accurate flight-path control may be required.
  - Phases: climb (CL), cruise (CR), loiter (LO), in-flight refueling as tanker (RT), descent (D), emergency descent (ED), emergency deceleration (DE), aerial delivery (AD).
- **Category C** (terminal): gradual maneuvers that usually need accurate flight-path control.
  - Phases: takeoff (TO), catapult takeoff (CT), approach (PA), wave-off/go-around (WO), landing (L).

### 1.5 Levels of flying qualities — p.4 (PDF 3)
Each numerical value in section 3 is "a minimum condition to meet one of three Levels".
- **Level 1:** flying qualities clearly adequate for the mission Flight Phase.
- **Level 2:** adequate to accomplish the mission Flight Phase, but with some increase in pilot workload, some degradation in mission effectiveness, or both.
- **Level 3:** the airplane can be controlled safely, but pilot workload is excessive, mission effectiveness is inadequate, or both. Category A phases can be terminated safely, and Category B and C phases can be completed.

### 6.7.1 Level definitions — p.85 (PDF 84)
- Level 1 means better than or equal to the Level 1 boundary.
- Level 2 means worse than Level 1 but no worse than the Level 2 boundary.
- Level 3 means worse than Level 2 but no worse than the Level 3 boundary.
- If one boundary is labelled "Level 1 and Level 2", anything outside it is at best Level 3.
- **Rule-engine consequence:** Level = the best Level whose boundary is satisfied. Anything worse than the Level 3 boundary is "worse than Level 3".

### 3.1.6 State of the airplane and 3.1.6.1–3.1.6.2.1 — p.5–6 (PDF 4–5)
- The Airplane State is the selected configuration plus the functional status of components, throttle, weight, inertias, c.g. and stores. Trim and control positions are not part of the State.
- **Airplane Normal States** have no failures. They are tabulated in the Table XIX format (6.2).
- **Airplane Failure States** are Normal States with one or more malfunctions.
- **Special Failure States** have extremely remote failure probability. They may be excluded from section 3 with procuring-activity approval.

### 3.1.7 Operational Flight Envelope — p.6 (PDF 5); Table I — p.7 (PDF 6)
- The Operational Flight Envelope bounds speed, altitude and load factor within which the airplane must operate to perform its missions.
- If no specific guidance is given, use the representative conditions in Table I.

### 3.1.8 Service Flight Envelope and 3.1.8.1–3.1.8.4 — p.6, 8 (PDF 5, 7)
- The contractor establishes it from airplane limits. It may coincide with the Operational envelope or lie outside it, but never inside it.
- **V_max** is the lowest of three speeds:
  - the maximum permissible speed;
  - a safe margin below intolerable buffet or vibration;
  - the maximum speed at MAT from which dives can be recovered at 2,000 ft MSL or higher.
- **V_min** is the highest of these speeds:
  - (a) "1.1 V_S";
  - (b) "V_S + 10 knots equivalent airspeed";
  - (c) the speed below which full nose-up pitch control and trim cannot hold straight, steady flight;
  - (d) the lowest speed at which level flight can be held with MRT;
  - (e) for Category C, the speed limited by visibility or by tail-strike attitude.
- **h_max** is the altitude at which "a rate of climb of 100 feet per minute" can be held with MAT.
- **n(+) and n(-)** are the lowest (or highest) of three values:
  - the structural limit load factor;
  - the stall-warning C_L limit (3.4.2.1.1.2);
  - the load factor at full nose-up (or nose-down) pitch control;
  - a safe margin below buffet or vibration.

### 3.1.9 Permissible Flight Envelope — p.8 (PDF 7)
It encompasses all regions where operation is allowable and possible, consistent with 3.1.10.3.3.

### 3.1.10 Application of Levels — p.9–10 (PDF 8–9)
- **3.1.10.1 and Table II, Airplane Normal States:** Level 1 inside the Operational Flight Envelope and Level 2 inside the Service Flight Envelope.
- **3.1.10.2 and Table III, Failure States:**

  | Probability per flight of encountering | Within Operational envelope | Within Service envelope |
  |---|---|---|
  | Level 2 after failure | "< 10^-2 per flight" | (blank) |
  | Level 3 after failure | "< 10^-4 per flight" | "< 10^-2 per flight" |

  - The calculation assumes all components run for the longest mission time, and that each failure occurs at the most critical point.
  - "In no case shall a Failure State (except an approved Special Failure State) degrade any flying quality parameter outside the Level 3 limit."
- **3.1.10.2.1:** Specific-failure requirements must be met assuming the failure has occurred, regardless of its probability.
- **3.1.10.3.1:** For ground operation and terminal phases at conditions outside the envelopes (for example at V_S or on the ground), apply Levels as if in the Operational Flight Envelope.
- **3.1.10.3.2:** Requirements that name no Level must be met under all failure conditions, except approved Special Failure States.
- **3.1.10.3.3:** From anywhere in the Permissible envelope it must be possible to return readily and safely to the Service envelope, without exceptional skill and regardless of failures.
- **3.1.12 (p.10):** The numbers apply to the parameters of an **equivalent classical system** fitted to the actual response. The procuring activity judges whether the match is adequate. This matters for any rule that takes frequencies and damping from a higher-order model.

## 2. Longitudinal requirements

### 3.2.1.1 Longitudinal static stability — p.11 (PDF 10)
- **Levels 1 and 2:** there shall be "no tendency for airspeed to diverge aperiodically" after a disturbance, with cockpit controls **fixed and free**.
- This is considered satisfied if pitch force and pitch position vary smoothly with airspeed and have stable local gradients. The test conditions are:
  - (a) trimmer and throttle not moved;
  - (b) "1g acceleration normal to the flight path";
  - (c) constant altitude;
  - over "±15 percent or ±50 knots equivalent airspeed, whichever is less" about trim, limited by the Service Flight Envelope.
- The requirement is also satisfied if the flight control system provides speed stability, even with zero gradients.
- "Stable" means nose-down control to fly faster and nose-up control to fly slower. Gradients inside the breakout band do not count.
- **Level 3:** may be relaxed with approval, but "In no event shall its time to double amplitude be less than 6 seconds". Combined with other Level 3 qualities, no static instability is allowed unless demonstrated safe.
- **3.2.1.1.1 Transonic relaxation limits:**
  - Levels 1–2, center-stick: no local gradient more unstable than "3 pounds per 0.01 M", and the force change must not exceed "10 pounds" in the unstable direction. Wheel limits are 5 lb per 0.01 M and 15 lb.
  - Level 3, center-stick: 6 lb per 0.01 M and 20 lb. Wheel limits are 10 lb per 0.01 M and 30 lb.
  - The relaxation does not apply to Level 1 in any phase that needs prolonged transonic operation.
- **3.2.1.1.2 (p.12):** Trim changes during rapid speed changes must not make the load factor hard to hold. This is qualitative.

### 3.2.1.2 Phugoid stability — p.12 (PDF 11)
- Level 1: "ζp at least 0.04".
- Level 2: "ζp at least 0".
- Level 3: "T2 at least 55 seconds".
- These apply with pitch control free and fixed. They need not be met transonically where 3.2.1.1.1 relaxation is used.

### 3.2.1.3 Flight-path stability — p.12 (PDF 11)
- Applies to the **landing approach** phase, Category C (PA). Airspeed is changed by pitch control only, with throttle not changed.
- The local slope of flight-path angle γ against true airspeed at V_o_min must be negative, or less positive than:
  - Level 1: "0.06 degrees/knot";
  - Level 2: "0.15 degrees/knot";
  - Level 3: "0.24 degrees/knot".
- Thrust is set for the normal glide path at V_o_min.
- **Extra condition:** the slope at 5 kt below V_o_min "shall not be more than 0.05 degrees per knot more positive than the slope at V_o_min". An illustrative sketch accompanies this.

### 3.2.2.1 Short-period response — p.13 (PDF 12)
- Applies to angle-of-attack response at roughly constant speed.
- Controls free and fixed.
- Responses of any magnitude.
- For nonlinear oscillations, each cycle is checked.

### 3.2.2.1.3 Residual oscillations — p.13 (PDF 12)
- These are sustained oscillations in calm air.
- For Levels 1 and 2 they are excessive if either:
  - normal acceleration at the pilot's station exceeds "±0.05g" in any phase; or
  - pitch attitude exceeds "±3 mils" in Category A phases that need precise attitude control.
- Applies with pitch control fixed and free.

### 3.2.2.2 Control feel in maneuvering flight — p.13 (PDF 12)
- In steady turns and pullups at constant speed, pitch attitude and α must show no aperiodic divergence, controls fixed or free.
- Incremental force and deflection must be in the same sense as needed to start the change: aft means more positive and forward means more negative.
- This holds across the service load-factor range of 3.1.8.4.

### 3.2.2.2.1 Control forces in maneuvering flight — p.17 (PDF 16); Table V p.18 (PDF 17)
- **Linearity range:** Class I, II and III run from .5 to ".5[n_o(+) + 1] or 3 whichever is less". **Class IV runs from 0** to that same maximum.
- Outside the range, a local gradient that differs from the average by more than "50 percent" is excessive. Larger increases are allowed above "0.85 n_L".
- All local gradients must be within Table V. F_s/n "should be near the Level 1 upper boundaries" when high frequency is combined with low damping.
- **Side-stick:** Table V gives no numbers. The contractor must show the gradients give suitable flying qualities. This matters for the F-16, which has a side stick.

- **3.3.2.2 Roll-rate oscillations (p.24):**
  - After a yaw-control-free step roll command, roll rate at the first minimum must have the same sign as, and be no less than a percentage of, the roll rate at the first peak.
  - Level 1: 60 % for Categories A and C, 25 % for Category B.
  - Level 2: 25 % for Categories A and C, 0 for Category B.
  - For all Levels, bank angle must always change in the commanded direction. The command is held until bank has changed "at least 90 degrees".
- **3.3.2.2.1:** p_osc/p_av must be within Figure 4 for Levels 1 and 2. This applies to steps up to the size that gives "60-degree bank angle change in 1.7T_d seconds".
- **3.3.2.3:** φ_osc/φ_av after a yaw-control-free impulse roll command must be within Figure 5 for Levels 1 and 2. The impulse should be as abrupt as practical.
- **3.3.2.4 Sideslip excursions (p.24):**
  - After a yaw-control-free step roll command, Δβ/k must be less than the values below. The command is held until bank has changed at least 90 deg.

  | Level | Category | Adverse (right roll gives right sideslip) | Proverse (right roll gives left sideslip) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | A | 6 degrees | 2 degrees |
  | 1 | B & C | 10 degrees | 3 degrees |
  | 2 | All | 15 degrees | 4 degrees |

- **3.3.2.4.1 (p.26):** For small inputs, sideslip must be within Figure 6 for Levels 1 and 2. This covers steps up to the size that gives a 60° bank change "within T_d or 2 seconds, whichever is longer".
- **3.3.2.5 (p.26):** In the 3.3.4 rolls, with coordination allowed, pedal force to hold zero sideslip must not exceed "50 pounds for Class IV airplanes in Flight Phase Category A, Level 1". The limit is "100 pounds for all other combinations".
- **3.3.2.6 Turn coordination (p.26):**
  - Steady coordinated turns in either direction using "60 degrees of bank for Class IV airplanes", with pedal force "not exceeding 40 pounds".
  - With yaw controls free: roll stick force "not exceeding 5 pounds", or roll wheel force not exceeding 10 lb.
  - This constitutes Levels 1 and 2, trimmed for wings-level straight flight.
- **3.3.3:** Lateral-directional PIO. This is qualitative.

### 3.3.4 Roll control effectiveness — p.26–27 (PDF 25–26)
- Roll performance is expressed as φ_t, the bank angle change in a given time. For Class IV it is specified in 3.3.4.1.
- Rolls from banked flight start coordinated. Commands go both right and left, from steady bank and from wings-level.
- Inputs are "abrupt", with time "measured from the initiation of control force application". Pitch control is fixed.
- "Yaw control pedals shall remain free for Class IV airplanes for Level 1", and for carrier-based Category C Levels 1 and 2. Otherwise pedals may be used to reduce sideslip that retards roll rate.
- For TO, the time may be scaled by the ratio of rolling inertia at takeoff to that at landing.

## 4. Metric definitions (6.2) — p.71–82 (PDF 70–81)
- **T₂, time to double amplitude (6.2.1, p.71):**
  - "T2 = −.693/ζω_n for oscillations".
  - "T2 = .693τ for first-order divergences". As printed, the sign convention for τ is not stated at this entry.
- **ζ_sp and ω_nsp (6.2.5, p.75):** damping ratio and undamped natural frequency of the short-period oscillation. ζ_p and ω_np are the same for the phugoid. n is normal acceleration or load factor at the c.g.
- **n_L, n(+/−), n_o(+/−) (p.76):**
  - n_L is the symmetrical flight limit load factor for a Normal State, from structural considerations.
  - n(+) and n(−) bound the Service envelope in the V-n diagram.
  - n_o(+) and n_o(−) bound the Operational envelope in the V-n diagram.
- **n/α (p.77):** "the steady-state normal acceleration change per unit change in angle of attack for an incremental pitch control deflection at constant speed (airspeed and Mach number)". Units are g/rad, as on the figure axes.
- **F_s/n (p.77):** gradient of steady-state pitch force against n at constant speed. γ = sin⁻¹(vertical speed / true airspeed).
- **τ_R (6.2.6, p.77):** "first-order roll mode time constant, positive for stable mode". λ_R = −1/τ_R.
  - The τ_S entry is printed with the same wording, "first-order roll mode time constant". It is evidently a misprint for the spiral mode. λ_S = −1/τ_S.
- **ω_nd, ζ_d and T_d (p.77):**
  - ω_nd and ζ_d are the Dutch roll undamped natural frequency and damping ratio.
  - "T_d = 2π/(ω_nd√(1−ζ_d²))".
  - ω_φ and ζ_φ belong to the numerator quadratic of φ/δ_AS.
- **ω_nRS and ζ_RS (p.78):** coupled roll-spiral frequency and damping.
- **φ_t (p.78):** bank angle change in time t, for the control input form of 3.3.4.
- **p_osc/p_av (p.78)**, where p₁, p₂ and p₃ are roll rates at the first, second and third peaks (Figures 14 and 15):
  - ζ_d ≤ 0.2: (p₁ + p₃ − 2p₂)/(p₁ + p₃ + 2p₂).
  - ζ_d > 0.2: (p₁ − p₂)/(p₁ + p₂).
- **φ_osc/φ_av (p.78):** the same forms using bank-angle peaks φ₁, φ₂ and φ₃, after a pedals-free impulse aileron command.
- **β and Δβ (p.78):**
  - β is positive for flow from the right.
  - Δβ is the "maximum change in sideslip occurring within 2 seconds or one half-period of the Dutch roll, whichever is greater" for a step roll command.
- **k (p.78, p.81):** k = (φ_t)command / (φ_t)requirement.
  - The requirement comes from 3.3.4 or 3.3.4.1 for the Class, Category and Level.
  - The command value is the bank angle reached in the stated time for the given step, with pedals as specified.
- **t_nβ (p.81):** time for the Dutch roll component of sideslip to reach the n-th local maximum for a right step or pulse, or the n-th minimum for a left one.
  - Time is measured from when the cockpit control passes half the commanded amplitude.
  - For pulses, it is measured from halfway through the pulse.
- **ψ_β (p.81):** "phase angle expressed as a lag for a cosine representation of the Dutch roll oscillation in sideslip".
  - Formula: ψ_β = −(360/T_d)·t_nβ + (n − 1)·360 degrees.
  - Choose peaks one or two roll-mode time constants after the input. If the trace ramps, remove the ramp by drawing a line through the ramping part (p.82 sketch).
- **Worked example (p.82):** t = 2.95 s and T_d = 3.5 give ψ_β = −303°.
  - It uses "Level 1 … Class IV airplane in the approach" with φ_t = 30 deg in 1 second, pedals free.
  - The k values are 0.30, 0.27, 0.23 and 0.20 for cases (a) to (d).
  - Note that Table IXb Category C Level 1 gives 1.1 s for 30°, not 1 s. The example is illustrative only.
- **ψ_p/β (p.81):** phase between roll rate and sideslip in the free Dutch roll. It is positive when p leads β by 0 to 180°.
- **|φ/β|_d (p.81):** ratio of the bank-angle and sideslip envelope amplitudes in the Dutch roll.
- **Speeds (6.2.2, p.72–74):**
  - V_S is the stall speed (EAS) at 1g, taken as the highest of the C_Lmax, uncommanded-motion and intolerable-buffet speeds. It is measured with the trim settings on p.73 and computed as V_S = V/√n_f when n ≠ 1 at stall.
  - V_max and V_min are defined in 3.1.8.1 and 3.1.8.2. V_o_max and V_o_min are defined in 3.1.7.
  - V_NRT, V_MRT and V_MAT are high speed in level flight at normal rated, military rated and maximum augmented thrust.
  - Combat ceiling is where rate of climb is 500 ft/min; cruising ceiling is 300 ft/min at NRT; service ceiling is 100 ft/min.

### 6.3 Figure-based boundaries that are not simple min/max, and how to encode them

**A. Short-period thumbprints, Figures 1–3 (3.2.2.1.1).**
- These reduce exactly to interval checks on two or three derived quantities:
  - CAP = ω_nsp²/(n/α), with min and max as tabulated;
  - an ω_nsp floor;
  - for Category C, an n/α floor.
- Pass for Level L if all three hold: CAP_min,L ≤ CAP ≤ CAP_max,L, ω_nsp ≥ ω_floor,L, and n/α ≥ (n/α)_floor,L.
- Straight-line extension outside n/α 1–100 is automatic, because CAP is constant along each line.
- **Graphical, not printed:**
  - the Figure 3 Level 1 ω floor, about 0.86 (bounded between 0.85 and 0.87);
  - the Figure 3 n/α edges, about 2.7 for Level 1 and 1.7 for Level 2;
  - the Figure 1 and Figure 3 Level 2 floor of 0.6, which lies on a grid line.
- For certification-grade use, confirm these against a clean copy of the spec, or against MIL-HDBK-1797 or MIL-STD-1797A, where the same boundaries are tabulated. I did not use those documents here.
- The Figure 3 Level 3 note "always greater than 0.6" is a strict inequality.

**B. Figures 4 and 5: p_osc/p_av and φ_osc/φ_av against ψ_β (3.3.2.2.1, 3.3.2.3).**
- The allowed maximum is a piecewise-linear function of ψ_β. Encode it as a lookup table, for example `interp1(psi_pts, ratio_pts, psi, 'linear')`, and check ratio ≤ f(ψ_β).
- **Prerequisites:**
  1. Compute ψ_β from t_nβ and T_d (6.2.6), using a sideslip peak one or two τ_R after the input, with any ramp removed. Wrap ψ_β into [−360, 0].
  2. Choose the abscissa scale from the phase ψ_p/β.
     - If p leads β by 45–225°, use ψ_β directly on the 0 to −360 scale.
     - Otherwise, use the lower scale, which is shifted by 180°. Plot at ψ_β − 180 (mod 360).
  3. Compute the ratio with the ζ_d ≤ 0.2 or > 0.2 formula.
  4. Apply the rule only for inputs up to the size limit, such as a 60° bank in 1.7·T_d for Figure 4.
- The breakpoints are approximate (±5°, ±0.02). Digitize them properly before relying on the ramps.

**C. Figure 6: Δβ/k against ψ_β (3.3.2.4.1).** This is also piecewise-linear. It needs k from the relevant Table IX requirement (6.2.6) and Δβ within max(2 s, T_d/2). The input-size limit is a 60° bank within max(T_d, 2 s).

**D. Formula bounds in Table V and 3.2.2.3.1.** These need airplane parameters n/α and n_L. They are encoded as expressions (`min_expr` and `max_expr`) using clamp logic:
- Level 1 maximum = min(28.0, max(240/(n/α), 56/(n_L−1))), with the footnote that it is 28.0 when n_L < 3.

**E. Table IX roll performance.** This is a lookup keyed by (Level, Category or Phase, speed range) giving (bank angle, time limit).
- The speed range is derived from V, V_o_min, V_o_max, V_min and V_max, with different definitions for Level 1 and for Levels 2–3.
- CO uses Tables IXc and IXd, and GA uses Table IXe. These take precedence over IXb.
- Rolls must start at 1g for IXc, and between 0.8 n_o(−) and 0.8 n_o(+) for IXd and IXe.
- Pedals are free for Class IV Level 1.

**F. Envelope-dependent Level assignment (3.1.10, Tables II and III).**
- This is a rule-engine layer, not a metric. Level 1 is required inside the Operational envelope and Level 2 inside the Service envelope for Normal States.
- Failure States are governed by the probability limits in Table III.
- The engine therefore needs envelope definitions (Table I and 3.1.8) to choose the required Level for each flight condition.

**G. Flight-path stability (3.2.1.3).** This needs the γ–V_true curve near V_o_min. Check the local slope at V_o_min, and the slope difference between V_o_min − 5 kt and V_o_min. It is a derivative-based rule, not a single state value.
